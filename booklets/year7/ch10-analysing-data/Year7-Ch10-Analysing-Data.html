  • interpret a variety of statistical graphs, including divided bar graphs, sector graphs and line graphs
  • identify graphs that are misleading
  • draw stem-and-leaf plots and dot plots
  • calculate the mean, mode, median and range of a set of data, including data shown on graphs and plots

Extension · Stretch yourself
Worked example
In a survey of 72 students, the Soccer sector of a sector graph has an angle of 90°. How many students chose soccer?
  1. A full circle is 360°, so 90° is 90/360 = ¼ of the circle
  2. ¼ of 72 = 72 ÷ 4 = 18

Answer: 18 students chose soccer

E1A survey of 240 people has a Tennis sector of 60°. How many chose tennis?
E2In a survey of 180 people, 45 chose pizza. What angle should the pizza sector be?
E3In a picture graph, one symbol = 8 students. How many students do 3¾ symbols show? How would you show 20 students?
Lesson summaryMissed the lesson? Start here.
Reading any graph
  1. Read the title: what is the graph about?
  2. Check the key or the scale.
  3. Use a ruler to line up bars and points with the scale.
  4. Answer with the right units (students, °C, %).
Level 1
In a picture graph, = 10 students. A row shows 3½ symbols. How many students is that?
  1. 3 full symbols = 3 × 10 = 30
  2. Half a symbol = 10 ÷ 2 = 5
  3. 30 + 5 = 35

Answer: 35 students

Level 2
A sector graph shows 80 people. The Surfing sector is a quarter of the circle. How many chose surfing?
  1. A quarter means divide by 4
  2. 80 ÷ 4 = 20

Answer: 20 people

Level 3
A divided bar graph shows 45% of 400 students catch the bus. How many students is that?
  1. 45% means 45 out of every 100
  2. 400 has 4 hundreds, so 45 × 4 = 180

Answer: 180 students

Extension
A 120° sector shows the results for 90 people. How many people is that?
  1. 120/360 = ⅓ of the circle
  2. ⅓ of 90 = 90 ÷ 3 = 30

Answer: 30 people

Worked example
A shop’s sales doubled. On a poster, the circle for this year has twice the width and twice the height of last year’s circle. How many times bigger does it look?
  1. The width is 2 times as big
  2. The height is 2 times as big
  3. The area (how big it looks) is 2 × 2 = 4 times as big
  4. Sales only doubled, so the picture exaggerates by 2 times

Answer: it looks 4 times bigger

Year 7 · Chapter 10 · 10.02 Misleading graphsSummary
Lesson summaryMissed the lesson? Start here.
Is this graph fair?
  1. Does the vertical axis start at 0 (or show a break ⚡)?
  2. Is the scale even (equal gaps = equal amounts)?
  3. Are the title and axis labels there?
  4. Is all the data shown, with bars and pictures the same width?
Level 1
A column graph’s vertical axis starts at 90. What is the problem?
  1. Bar heights are measured from 90, not from 0
  2. So small differences look huge

Answer: it is misleading. The axis should start at 0 or show a break.

Level 2
Scores of 60 and 64 are drawn on an axis starting at 58. How many times as tall does the 64 bar look? What is the real difference?
  1. Heights on the graph: 60 − 58 = 2 and 64 − 58 = 6
  2. 6 ÷ 2 = 3, so the bar looks 3 times as tall
  3. Real difference: 64 − 60 = 4

Answer: it looks 3 times as tall, but the real difference is only 4

Level 3
Redraw the Level 2 graph fairly.
  1. Start the vertical axis at 0 with an even scale
  2. Plot the same values: 60 and 64
  3. Add a title and label both axes
  4. Make every bar the same width

Answer: the bars now look almost the same, which is the truth

Extension
A picture is drawn twice as wide and twice as tall. How many times bigger does it look?
  1. Area grows by width × height
  2. 2 × 2 = 4

Answer: 4 times bigger, even though the value only doubled

Drawing a dot plot
  1. Find the smallest and largest values.
  2. Draw a number line with an even scale.

The mean age of four friends is 13. A fifth friend joins and the mean age becomes 14. How old is the new friend?

  2. Total of five ages: 5 × 14 = 70

Worked example: combining groups
Class A has 10 students with a mean of 70. Class B has 15 students with a mean of 80. What is the mean of all 25 students?
  1. Total for A: 10 × 70 = 700
  2. Total for B: 15 × 80 = 1200
  3. Total for everyone: 700 + 1200 = 1900
  4. Mean: 1900 ÷ 25 = 76

Answer: 76 (not 75, because Class B is bigger)

Comparing data sets
  1. Centre (mean or median): which group is higher?
  2. Spread (range): smaller range = more consistent.
  3. Look for outliers that change the mean.
  4. Primary data you collect; secondary data someone else collected.

In a back-to-back plot, what does the left leaf 3 on stem 2 stand for?

  2. Stem 2, leaf 3

Level 2
Medians: Class X 64, Class Y 71. Ranges: X 20, Y 45. Compare.
  1. Y has the higher median, so Y did better
  2. X has the smaller range, so X is more consistent

Answer: Y higher, X more consistent

Level 3
Amy: mean 13.3, range 3. Beth: mean 14, range 17. Who is more reliable?
  1. Means are close
  2. Amy’s range is much smaller

Answer: Amy is more reliable
