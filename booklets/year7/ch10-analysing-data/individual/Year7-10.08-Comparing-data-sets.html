Worked example: combining groups
Class A has 10 students with a mean of 70. Class B has 15 students with a mean of 80. What is the mean of all 25 students?
  1. Total for A: 10 × 70 = 700
  2. Total for B: 15 × 80 = 1200
  3. Total for everyone: 700 + 1200 = 1900
  4. Mean: 1900 ÷ 25 = 76

Answer: 76 (not 75, because Class B is bigger)

Lesson summaryMissed the lesson? Start here.
Comparing data sets
  1. Centre (mean or median): which group is higher?
  2. Spread (range): smaller range = more consistent.
  3. Look for outliers that change the mean.
  4. Primary data you collect; secondary data someone else collected.
Level 1
In this back-to-back plot, what does the left leaf 3 on stem 2 stand for?
  1. Left leaves belong to the left group (Team A)
  2. Stem 2 and leaf 3 make 23
Team AStemTeam B
7  321  5
430  2  6

Key: 2 | 3 = 23

Answer: a score of 23 for Team A

Level 2
Medians: Class X 64, Class Y 71. Ranges: X 20, Y 45. Compare the classes.
  1. Y has the higher median, so Y did better on average
  2. X has the smaller range, so X is more consistent

Answer: Y scored higher; X was more consistent

Level 3
Amy: mean 13.3, range 3. Beth: mean 14, range 17. Who is more reliable?
  1. The means are close
  2. Amy’s range is much smaller, so her scores are more alike

Answer: Amy is more reliable

Extension
4 values have a mean of 5 and 6 values have a mean of 10. What is the combined mean?
  1. Totals: 4 × 5 = 20 and 6 × 10 = 60
  2. Combined: 80 ÷ 10 = 8

Answer: 8 (not 7.5, because the second group is bigger)
