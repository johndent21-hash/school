Worked example: split stems
Many values share a stem: 41, 42, 42, 44, 45, 46, 47, 47, 48, 49, 52, 53. Draw a split-stem plot.
  1. Split each stem into two rows
  2. Row “4” holds leaves 0 to 4; row “4*” holds leaves 5 to 9
  3. Do the same for 5 if you need to

52  3

Key: 4* | 7 = 47

Answer: the long row is split, so it is easier to read

Lesson summaryMissed the lesson? Start here.
Drawing a stem-and-leaf plot
  1. Choose the stems (usually the tens) and write them down the left.
  2. Write each leaf (the units) next to its stem.
  3. Rewrite with the leaves in order, smallest first.
  4. Always add a key, e.g. 3 | 5 = 35.
Level 1
What values are in the 3 row?
  1. The stem 3 means 30-something
  2. Put each leaf after the stem: 32, 35, 37

Key: 3 | 2 = 32

Answer: 32, 35 and 37

Extension
Why split a stem into 4 and 4*?
  1. A row with lots of leaves is hard to read
  2. 4 takes leaves 0–4 and 4* takes 5–9

Answer: it spreads out crowded data
