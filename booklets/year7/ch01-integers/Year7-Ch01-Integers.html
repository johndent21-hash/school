How to use this booklet

1Each lesson starts with learning intentions and success criteria. Write the key terms as your teacher explains them, and use the notes box.
2Work through the levels in order: Level 1 , then Level 2, then Level 3. Extension questions stretch you further.
WE DOGrey boxes with a dark border. Work with your teacher. Copy what they write on the board.
YOU DOBlue boxes. Work on your own. Show your working in the box and write your answer.
3Some questions ask you to draw a diagram, graph or construction. Use the space given, a ruler and a sharp pencil.
4At the end of each lesson, show off your skill: choose one question, then tear off the ticket and hand it in.
5Missed a lesson? Each lesson ends with a summary that has a worked example for every level. Start there, then try the questions.

In this chapter you will

  • understand positive and negative numbers and their opposites
  • use a number line to show and compare integers
  • order integers
  • add, subtract, multiply and divide integers
  • use the order of operations with integers, with and without a calculator
  • solve problems involving integers

NSW Mathematics K–10 Syllabus (2022), Stage 4 Number and algebra: MA4-INT-C-01 compares, orders and calculates with integers to solve problems.

The opposite of n is written −n. If n = −4, what is −n?

  2. The opposite of −4 is 4

Lesson summaryMissed the lesson? Start here.
Remember
  1. Integers are whole numbers: …, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, …
  2. Negative numbers are below zero.
  3. The opposite is the same distance from 0 on the other side.
  4. 0 is its own opposite.
Level 1
Write −7 as a real situation.
  1. Negative means below or less

Answer: 7°C below zero

Level 2
Opposite of −23?
  1. Same distance, other side of 0

Answer: 23

Level 3
Which integers are 4 from 0?
  1. One on each side of 0

Answer: 4 and −4

Extension
If n = −6, find −n.
  1. Opposite of −6

Answer: 6

Lesson summaryMissed the lesson? Start here.
Remember
  1. Numbers increase to the right.
  2. Negatives are left of 0.
  3. Use an even scale: equal gaps.
  4. “Between” does not include the end numbers.
Level 1
Where is −3?
  1. 3 steps left of 0

Integers between −2 and 2?
  1. Do not include −2 and 2

Level 3
Mark −20 on a scale of 10s
  1. Each gap is 10
  2. 2 gaps left of 0

Answer: second mark left of 0

Extension
Halfway between −6 and 2?
  1. Distance 8, half is 4
  2. −6 + 4

Answer: −2

Lesson summaryMissed the lesson? Start here.
Remember
  1. Further right on the number line = larger.
  2. < means “is less than”; > means “is greater than”.
  3. Ascending: smallest to largest.
  4. Descending: largest to smallest.
Level 1
Is −5 < −1?
  1. −5 is further left

Answer: True

Level 2
Order −2, 4, −8, 1
  1. Smallest is the most negative

Answer: −8, −2, 1, 4

Level 3
Descending: −3, −30, 3, 0
  1. Largest first

Answer: 3, 0, −3, −30

Extension
Order −(−4), −6, 2
  1. −(−4) = 4

Answer: −6, 2, 4

Worked example
Find the missing number: ☐ + (−14) = −5
  1. Think: what plus −14 gives −5?
  2. Undo by adding 14: −5 + 14 = 9
  3. Check: 9 + (−14) = −5 ✓

Answer: 9

E2−13 + ☐ + 6 = 0
Lesson summaryMissed the lesson? Start here.
Remember
  1. Adding a positive: move right.
  2. Adding a negative: move left.
  3. Same signs: add and keep the sign.
  4. Different signs: subtract, keep the sign of the bigger number.
Level 1
4 + (−6)
  1. Start at 4, move 6 left

Answer: −2

Level 2
−5 + (−3)
  1. Same signs: 5 + 3 = 8, keep −

Answer: −8

Level 3
−9 + 4 + (−2)
  1. −9 + 4 = −5
  2. −5 + (−2) = −7

Answer: −7

Extension
☐ + (−4) = 1
  1. 1 + 4 = 5

Answer: 5
